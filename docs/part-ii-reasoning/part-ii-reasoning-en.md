## Part II Reasoning

{1}------------------------------------------------

![](assets/_page_1_Picture_0.jpeg)

{2}------------------------------------------------

This part focuses on the reasoning capabilities of agents and how they use them to learn. The previous part showed how a basic next-token LLM is trained. Agentic systems typically use more advanced reasoning and multimodal models, which we continue to call *LLMs*. Here, we discuss why these models are needed and how they are trained and used.

The first chapter introduces reasoning models. Early LLMs struggled with even simple reasoning problems, and chain-of-thought prompting marked a major advance. We discuss mathematical word problems, chain-of-thought prompting, and in-context learning, along with models that use self-reflection to improve their behavior. Self-reflection loops also led to the development of agentic harnesses for LLMs.

The next chapter goes beyond language to show how the Transformer architecture also applies to vision and action. It introduces different kinds of world models, which help agents act more intelligently as they interact with the world.

The final chapter introduces metalearning, a paradigm underlying many forms of learning in agentic LLMs. We discuss what metalearning is and why it is challenging.

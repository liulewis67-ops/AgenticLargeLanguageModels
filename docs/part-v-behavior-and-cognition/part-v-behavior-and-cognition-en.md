## Part V Behavior and Cognition

{1}------------------------------------------------

![](assets/_page_1_Picture_0.jpeg)

{2}------------------------------------------------

In the previous parts, we examined agentic LLMs primarily through a technical lens: their architectures, training procedures, reasoning strategies, tool use, and applications. This part shifts the focus from how agentic LLMs work and are built to how they *behave*, especially in social settings. As these systems are increasingly deployed as assistants, collaborators, and tutors, their behavioral tendencies have practical consequences. For example, it matters whether an agent tends to cooperate or defect in strategic situations, responds to politeness, or tracks another agent's beliefs in a multi-agent simulation.

Most of the research in this part builds on methods originally developed to study humans and other animals, including research on strategic behavior, social etiquette, empathy, and personality. Each chapter ends with a brief reflection on whether these methods can be usefully applied to AI.

In the next and final part, we go deeper into questions of LLM inner workings and the potential emergence of various forms of cognition, or even consciousness.
